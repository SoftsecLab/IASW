\paragraph{Local-DP hyperparameter sensitivity.}
The Local-DP alignment score (Eq.~\eqref{eq:iasw_score}) depends on two
hyperparameters: the gap penalty $\delta_{\mathrm{DP}}$ and the activation-area
normalization exponent $\alpha$. We sweep
$\delta_{\mathrm{DP}} \in \{0, -0.01, -0.02, -0.05, -0.10, -0.20\}$ against
$\alpha \in \{1, \tfrac12\}$ on C4, Wiki, and Dolly, selecting the
configuration that maximizes mean TPR@1\%FPR over the 9 attack suite
(human negative pool, LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct dual stream).

Two observations emerge. \textbf{First}, the normalization exponent is
cross-domain consistent: $\alpha=\tfrac12$ (square-root activation area) is
optimal on all three datasets. \textbf{Second}, the optimal gap penalty splits
the datasets: $\delta_{\mathrm{DP}}=-0.2$ on C4 and Dolly, but
$\delta_{\mathrm{DP}}=-0.05$ on Wiki. Wiki texts have longer, more
semantically diffuse sentences; the stricter $-0.2$ gap prematurely truncates
the local alignment path.

\paragraph{Parameter mismatch and adaptation.}
Table~\ref{tab:localdp_mismatch} quantifies the cost of using the wrong gap.
Applying $\delta_{\mathrm{DP}}=-0.05$ to C4 or Dolly drops TPR@1\%FPR by
$15.2$ and $10.4$ points, respectively; applying $\delta_{\mathrm{DP}}=-0.2$
to Wiki drops it by $9.9$ points. The mismatch is asymmetric: the stricter
gap $-0.2$ is a safer default, incurring smaller loss on mismatched domains
than the looser gap $-0.05$.

We therefore adopt a two-stage adaptation strategy.
\textit{Stage 1 (zero-shot default):} a new domain is scored with
$\delta_{\mathrm{DP}}=-0.2, \alpha=\tfrac12$ without any calibration.
\textit{Stage 2 (lightweight tuning):} when a few hundred unlabeled human
samples are available, we evaluate the held-out text under
$\delta_{\mathrm{DP}} \in \{-0.05, -0.2\}$ and retain the configuration with
higher empirical TPR@1\%FPR; the exponent $\alpha$ is left at
$\tfrac12$ because it is consistent across datasets. When more validation
data is available, the search can be expanded to the full six-value gap grid
at negligible cost, since all scoring reuses cached similarity matrices and
requires no GPU recomputation. When no labeled validation set is available,
the dual/standalone score fusion already provides a fallback that attenuates
gap mismatch without additional tuning.
